01 — Nothing under it

Most coins launch with nothing under them.

One dev, one snipe, one dump. A fresh coin on pump.fun starts on an empty curve: whoever sells first takes everything that was put in, and the price can fall all the way back to where it began.

07 — Questions

Asked and answered.

What exactly happens when I launch?

You sign one transaction in your own wallet. In order, it creates your coin on pump.fun, buys it three times with 0.5 SOL each from the platform's pool, burns every token those buys received, closes the pool's token account, and then (if you chose one) makes your own buy of up to 0.5 SOL. When your buy doesn't fit in the same transaction, it rides in a second one, sent together with the first as a Jito bundle so it lands right behind the backing.

Why can't the 1.5 SOL ever leave?

On a bonding curve, SOL comes out only when tokens are sold back in. The backing bought tokens and burned them, so those tokens no longer exist and can never be sold. Everyone else can sell what they bought, but the SOL the burned tokens paid for stays in the curve, and moves with it into the pool when the coin graduates.

Does a floor mean I can't lose money?

No. The floor is the price the curve can't fall below, and it sits a little above the launch price. If you buy higher than the floor, you can still lose most of what you put in. Memecoins are extremely risky. Nothing here is financial advice.

Who pays for the backing?

The platform's pool, a public wallet you can watch above. You pay pump.fun's normal creation cost, network fees and your own optional buy. Azuro adds no fee to your launch.

Can I buy my own coin at launch?

Yes, up to 0.5 SOL, and always after the backing. You can't buy before it: the backing is first in the launch transaction.

Why one ticker and one image?

So nobody can copy a coin that is doing well. A ticker or an image already used on Azuro is refused, including images that are only slightly changed.

What if the pool runs dry?

Then launching pauses. The site shows how many launches the pool can still back, and the terminal won't let anyone launch a coin it can't back.

Is Azuro part of pump.fun?

No. Azuro is independent. Every coin is created and traded on pump.fun's own program, and graduates to pump.fun's pool like any other coin.
